\begin{abstract}
Automated road surface inspection is vital for transportation safety, infrastructure maintenance, and autonomous navigation. Modern mobile mapping and vehicular sensor platforms increasingly capture ultra-high-definition 4K ($3840 \times 2160$) imagery, theoretically enabling the detection of early-stage, microscopic road distresses. However, detecting hazardous ``ultra-fine'' potholes (bounding box area $S < 32^2\text{ px}^2$, occupying less than $0.012\%$ of the image area) poses a severe operational dilemma. In this paper, we conduct the first systematic multi-paradigm empirical study on the canonical HRP4K benchmark (6,003 images, 900 test images) comparing brute-force architectural scaling, patch slicing frameworks, and auxiliary scale-specialized feature heads. We uncover the \textit{``4K Illusion''}: feeding native 4K frames into state-of-the-art Transformer detectors (RT-DETR-L) inflates computational latency by $5.1\times$ ($220.4\text{ ms}$, $4.5\text{ FPS}$) while paradoxically degrading detection accuracy ($58.07\% \text{ AP}_{50}$ vs. $62.48\%$ at 2K) due to feature dispersion and gradient sparsity. Conversely, popular spatial slicing methods (SAHI, Sliced-NMS) cause severe contextual fragmentation, catastrophic false positive explosions ($\text{FPPI} \ge 1.08$), and multi-second execution delays ($920 - 3622\text{ ms}$). To resolve this paradox, we propose \textbf{P2-DETR}, a lightweight, real-time dual-branch architecture that couples a frozen 2K Transformer detector ($32.8\text{M}$ parameters) with a trainable dense auxiliary $P_2$ head ($stride=4$, $2.98\text{M}$ parameters, $480 \times 480$ feature map). We overcome extreme foreground/background imbalance ($99.83\%$ background cells) via Sigmoid Focal Loss and eliminate cross-scale suppression using \textit{Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS}. Our framework shatters the ultra-fine ceiling, achieving \textbf{93.22\%--94.07\% Ultra-Fine Recall} (+2.12\% over native 2K), while reducing post-processing latency to \textbf{4.24~ms} and sustaining real-time throughput ($>20\text{ FPS}$). Comprehensive ablation studies dissect the mathematical breakdown of Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF) on asymmetric multi-resolution branches and establish Scale-Partitioned NMS as the Pareto-optimal post-processing mechanism.

\keywords{Pothole Detection \and High-Resolution 4K Imagery \and Ultra-Fine Objects \and Real-Time DETR \and Scale-Specialized Feature Fusion \and Intelligent Transportation Systems.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Pavement degradation, particularly the emergence of potholes, poses severe hazards to vehicular safety, inflates municipal infrastructure maintenance budgets, and induces catastrophic structural damage to automotive suspensions and tires \cite{arya2021deep,maeda2018road}. The World Health Organization estimates that compromised road surfaces contribute substantially to traffic accidents and vehicle repair costs exceeding hundreds of billions of dollars annually. Consequently, municipal road administration authorities and autonomous vehicle developers urgently seek automated, real-time computer vision systems capable of continuous, millimeter-level road distress assessment from mobile inspection vehicles \cite{fan2019pothole,du2020pothole}.

With the rapid deployment of high-resolution digital cameras on vehicular dashcams, uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs), and specialized pavement inspection vans, 4K Ultra-High-Definition ($3840 \times 2160\text{ px}$) imagery has become widely accessible. The primary operational objective of acquiring high-resolution video is detecting nascent, early-stage distresses before they expand into vehicle-damaging craters. In the canonical HRP4K benchmark \cite{hrp4k_dataset}, over $51.2\%$ of all annotated ground-truth potholes fall into the \textit{Ultra-Fine} scale category (bounding box area $S < 32^2 = 1024\text{ px}^2$), occupying as little as $0.005\% - 0.012\%$ of the total pixel area. While 4K imagery physically captures these microscopic distresses, extracting them reliably under strict real-time vehicular operating constraints exposes fundamental limitations in contemporary computer vision paradigms.

Currently, high-resolution inspection literature is divided into two competing paradigms, both of which suffer from fatal drawbacks in real-world deployment (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigm_comparison}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Brute-force Resolution Scaling}: Downscaling 4K inputs to standard legacy resolutions ($640 \times 640$) causes severe spatial quantization: an ultra-fine $20 \times 20$ pothole in 4K shrinks to less than $3.3 \times 3.3$ pixels, completely disappearing within the downsampling stride of standard Feature Pyramid Networks ($stride \ge 8$). Conversely, feeding full 4K frames into modern Transformer detectors (e.g., RT-DETR-L \cite{lv2023detrs}) exposes what we term the \textit{``4K Illusion''}: self-attention and multi-scale feature cross-attention explode quadratically, ballooning per-image latency to $220.4\text{ ms}$ ($4.5\text{ FPS}$) and VRAM allocation beyond $18\text{ GB}$. Crucially, as shown in our benchmarks, overall detection accuracy paradoxically collapses ($58.07\% \text{ AP}_{50}$ vs. $62.48\%$ at 2K) due to extreme gradient sparsity and feature dilution over massive uninformative asphalt regions.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Patch Decomposition and Slicing}: Popular frameworks such as Slicing Aided Hyper Inference (SAHI) \cite{akyon2022sahi} partition the 4K canvas into 25--32 overlapping crops. However, patch slicing obliterates road perspective geometry and lane boundaries. Potholes sliced across tile borders suffer severe spatial clipping, while identical pavement textures duplicated across neighboring tiles cause catastrophic false positive explosions ($\text{FPPI} \ge 1.08 - 19.23$). Most critically, sequential or batch inference across 32 patches inflates inference latency to $920 - 3622\text{ ms}$ per 4K frame, totally precluding real-time vehicular navigation ($20 - 30\text{ FPS}$).
\end{enumerate}

To conquer this high-resolution dilemma without incurring prohibitive compute overhead, we introduce \textbf{P2-DETR}, an asymmetric, dual-branch architecture. We freeze a pre-trained 2K Real-Time Detection Transformer (RT-DETR-L \cite{lv2023detrs}) to preserve macro-level context (lane marks, road curvature, large potholes) and augment it with an ultra-lightweight, trainable auxiliary $P_2$ head ($stride=4$, $2.98\text{M}$ parameters) operating directly on high-resolution $480 \times 480$ feature maps. To overcome the extreme class imbalance ($99.83\%$ background cells), we formulate a balanced Sigmoid Focal Loss training objective. Furthermore, we investigate post-processing fusion, dissecting why classical ensemble techniques like Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF) \cite{solovyev2021weighted} fail on multi-resolution branches, and introduce \textit{Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS}, which filters out cross-scale interference and slashes post-processing latency by more than $50\%$.

Our primary technical contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Paradigm Benchmark on HRP4K}: We present the first comprehensive, dual-mode evaluation (Academic Protocol $\text{conf}=0.001$ and Operational Protocol $\text{conf}=0.25$) comparing architectural scaling, patch slicing (SAHI, Sliced-NMS, Perspective Grid), and dense feature fusion across all 900 held-out test images of the HRP4K benchmark.
    \item \textbf{The P2-DETR Dual-Branch Framework}: We design a frozen 2K Transformer backbone coupled with a lightweight trainable $P_2$ auxiliary head ($stride=4$, $+2.98\text{M}$ parameters), which boosts Ultra-Fine Recall from $91.10\%$ to $93.22\% - 94.07\%$ with minimal computational cost.
    \item \textbf{Training and Loss Function Ablation}: We systematically dissect target assignment ($1\times 1$ vs. $3\times 3$) and loss formulations (BCE vs. Quality Focal Loss (QFL) \cite{li2020generalized} vs. Sigmoid Focal Loss), proving that Pure Focal Loss ($\alpha=0.25, \gamma=2.0$) on single-cell assignment prevents gradient noise on dense feature maps.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS}: We demonstrate empirically and theoretically that Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF) breaks down on cross-resolution branches ($AP_{50} < 4\%$). In contrast, our Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS isolates ultra-fine priors, achieving peak Ultra-fine $AP_{50}$ ($50.39\%$), lowest FPPI ($1.4967$), and a $2.26\times$ latency reduction ($4.24\text{ ms}$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Pavement Defect and Road Distress Detection}
Early automated road distress inspection relied on handcrafted texture descriptors, shadow thresholding, and stereo-vision disparity modeling \cite{fan2019pothole,du2020pothole}. With the advent of deep convolutional neural networks, benchmark challenges such as the Global Road Damage Detection (GRDDC) \cite{arya2021deep,maeda2018road} propelled models based on Faster R-CNN \cite{ren2015faster} and the YOLO series (YOLOv3 \cite{redmon2018yolov3}, YOLOv4 \cite{bochkovskiy2020yolov4}, YOLO11 \cite{ultralytics2024yolo11}). However, existing datasets predominantly feature low-resolution imagery ($512 \times 512$ or $720\text{p}$) with large, severe potholes, obscuring the challenge of detecting microscopic, nascent distresses in high-speed mobile inspection. The release of the HRP4K dataset \cite{hrp4k_dataset} established the first large-scale 4K benchmark, revealing that conventional detectors experience sharp performance degradation when scaled to ultra-fine targets.

\subsection{Small and Tiny Object Detection}
Detecting tiny objects in high-resolution aerial and surveillance imagery has spurred extensive research \cite{yang2019clustered,wang2021high}. Standard Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) \cite{lin2017feature} and bidirectional pyramids such as BiFPN \cite{tan2020efficientdet} construct hierarchical representations where bottom-up strides ($stride \ge 8$) severely downsample spatial detail. To recover tiny targets, high-resolution representation networks (HRNet) \cite{wang2021high} maintain parallel high-resolution streams, but their massive memory footprint precludes real-time inference on 4K imagery. Several works introduce auxiliary high-resolution heads ($P_2$, $stride=4$) in convolutional detectors \cite{bochkovskiy2020yolov4}. However, integrating dense $P_2$ representations into real-time Transformer architectures without destabilizing attention mechanisms remains an open challenge.

\subsection{Spatial Decomposition and Slicing Strategies}
To bypass GPU memory limits during high-resolution inference, patch-based inference frameworks such as SAHI \cite{akyon2022sahi} and Clustered Slicing \cite{yang2019clustered} divide the canvas into overlapping sub-images. Each tile is evaluated independently, followed by coordinate mapping and Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS). While effective for isolated aerial targets (e.g., small vehicles on uniform water/grass backgrounds), slicing road imagery induces severe boundary truncations, duplicates ambiguous crack textures across adjoining tiles, and compounds false positives, leading to multi-second latencies that are unsuitable for real-time vehicular navigation.

\subsection{Real-Time Detection Transformers (RT-DETR)}
Originating from attention mechanisms in natural language processing \cite{vaswani2017attention} and hierarchical Vision Transformers \cite{liu2021swin}, DETR \cite{carion2020end} and Deformable DETR \cite{zhu2020deformable} eliminated handcrafted components like anchor generation and NMS by formulating detection as direct set prediction. However, their high computational complexity hindered real-time adoption. Lv et al. \cite{lv2023detrs} introduced RT-DETR, which combines an efficient Hybrid Encoder with multi-scale feature interactions and an uncertainty-minimal query selection mechanism, outperforming YOLO detectors in accuracy and latency. In this work, we exploit RT-DETR-L as our primary macro-detector and resolve its sub-grid spatial blindness via scale-specialized dense feature gating.

\section{Methodology}

The architecture of \textbf{P2-DETR} is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture_pipeline}. It consists of three tightly coupled modules: (1) a frozen macro-level Transformer detector operating on 2K resolution, (2) an auxiliary dense $P_2$ feature head ($stride=4$) trained with class-imbalance focal objectives, and (3) a Scale-Partitioned Prior Non-Maximum Suppression gating mechanism.

\subsection{Frozen Macro-Level Transformer Backbone}
Let an input image be denoted by $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}$, where $H=1080$ and $W=1920$ define the 2K canonical resolution. The image is processed by the RT-DETR-L backbone (HGNetv2 / ResNet-50 \cite{he2016deep}) to produce multi-scale feature hierarchies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F} = \{ \mathbf{C}_3, \mathbf{C}_4, \mathbf{C}_5 \}, \quad \text{with strides } \{ 8, 16, 32 \},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{C}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{C_k \times \frac{H}{2^k} \times \frac{W}{2^k}}$. These features are passed through an Efficient Hybrid Encoder combining Cross-Scale Feature Interaction (CCFM) and Intra-Scale Self-Attention (AIFI) to produce enriched feature representations $\mathbf{P}_3, \mathbf{P}_4, \mathbf{P}_5$. An uncertainty-minimal query selection decoder outputs $K=300$ macro-level candidate detections:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{Nat} = \big\{ (\mathbf{b}_k, s_k) \mid k = 1, \dots, K \big\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{b}_k = (x_k, y_k, w_k, h_k)$ represents normalized bounding box coordinates and $s_k \in [0, 1]$ is the classification confidence score. Crucially, all parameters of the 2K backbone, hybrid encoder, and decoder ($\approx 32.8\text{M}$ parameters) remain strictly \textbf{frozen} during training. This preserves pretrained road representations and prevents catastrophic forgetting.

\subsection{Dense $P_2$ Feature Reconstruction Head}
Because the finest feature map in standard RT-DETR ($\mathbf{P}_3$, $stride=8$) downsamples a $20 \times 20$ pothole to a tiny $2.5 \times 2.5$ region, spatial localization collapses. To resolve this, we construct an auxiliary $P_2$ branch with stride $s=4$, producing a dense spatial lattice of dimensions $\frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4} = 270 \times 480 = 129,600$ grid locations.

The $P_2$ feature representation is constructed by directly tapping the early low-level backbone stage $\mathbf{C}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{C_{c2} \times \frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4}}$ (where $C_{c2} = 128$) and passing it through a dedicated, lightweight two-stage convolution adapter:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}_2 = \text{SiLU}\Big( \text{BN}\big( \text{Conv}_{3\times 3}( \text{SiLU}( \text{BN}( \text{Conv}_{1\times 1}(\mathbf{C}_2) ) ) ) \big) \Big) \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times \frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4}}.
\end{equation}
By directly adapting $\mathbf{C}_2$ without modifying the frozen hybrid encoder, the native detector remains completely untouched.

On top of $\mathbf{P}_2$, an anchor-free lightweight prediction head branches into two parallel convolutional streams:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Classification Subnetwork}: Two $3 \times 3$ convolutional layers (with BatchNorm and SiLU) followed by a $1 \times 1$ convolution predicting classification logits $\hat{\mathbf{p}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times \frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4}}$. The final bias is initialized to $-4.59$ to account for class imbalance prior ($p=0.01$).
    \item \textbf{Bounding Box Regression Subnetwork}: Two $3 \times 3$ convolutional layers (with BatchNorm and SiLU) followed by a $1 \times 1$ convolution and ReLU activation predicting non-negative pixel distance offsets $\hat{\mathbf{o}} = (\hat{l}, \hat{t}, \hat{r}, \hat{b}) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{4 \times \frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4}}$.
\end{enumerate}
For each grid cell $(i, j)$ with center $(x_c, y_c) = \big((j + 0.5)s, (i + 0.5)s\big)$, the decoded bounding box coordinates $[\hat{x}_1, \hat{y}_1, \hat{x}_2, \hat{y}_2]$ in image space are parameterized as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \hat{x}_1 &= \max(0, x_c - \hat{l}_{ij}), \quad \hat{y}_1 = \max(0, y_c - \hat{t}_{ij}), \\
    \hat{x}_2 &= \min(W, x_c + \hat{r}_{ij}), \quad \hat{y}_2 = \min(H, y_c + \hat{b}_{ij}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This anchor-free formulation avoids sensitive scale heuristics and exponential instabilities, ensuring robust boundary regression for microscopic potholes. The auxiliary adapter and head require only $2.98\text{M}$ parameters, representing less than $9.1\%$ of the base model budget.

\subsection{Training Formulation for Extreme Class Imbalance}
On a $270 \times 480$ feature map, there are $129,600$ candidate anchors per image. With an average of only $1.02$ ground-truth potholes per image, positive grid cells account for less than $0.0017\%$ of all anchors (an extreme background-to-foreground ratio exceeding $50,000 : 1$). Under standard Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE), easy background gradients drown out the sparse foreground signals, causing the detector to collapse into near-zero confidence predictions.

To guarantee robust convergence without gradient saturation, we formulate the training objective with Sigmoid Focal Loss \cite{lin2017focal}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{cls} = -\frac{1}{N_{pos}} \sum_{i, j} \alpha_t (1 - p_{t, ij})^\gamma \log(p_{t, ij}),
\end{equation}
where $p_{t, ij} = \hat{p}_{ij}$ if $y_{ij} = 1$ and $p_{t, ij} = 1 - \hat{p}_{ij}$ otherwise. We set focusing parameter $\gamma = 2.0$ and balancing factor $\alpha = 0.25$.

Bounding box regression is supervised strictly on positive cells using composite $\ell_1$ distance loss on spatial offsets and Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU) loss \cite{rezatofighi2019generalized}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{box} = \frac{1}{N_{pos}} \sum_{y_{ij}=1} \|\hat{\mathbf{o}}_{ij} - \mathbf{o}_{ij}^*\|_1, \quad
    \mathcal{L}_{giou} = \frac{1}{N_{pos}} \sum_{y_{ij}=1} \big(1 - \text{GIoU}(\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{ij}, \mathbf{b}_{ij}^*)\big),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{o}_{ij}^* = (x_c - x_1^*, y_c - y_1^*, x_2^* - x_c, y_2^* - y_c)$ represents ground truth distance offsets. The total training loss is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{total} = \lambda_{cls} \mathcal{L}_{cls} + \lambda_{box} \mathcal{L}_{box} + \lambda_{giou} \mathcal{L}_{giou},
\end{equation}
with loss balancing coefficients set to $\lambda_{cls} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{box} = 2.0$, and $\lambda_{giou} = 2.0$.

\subsection{Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS}
A common post-processing strategy in multi-head networks is concatenating all predictions and applying Greedy NMS \cite{neubeck2006efficient} or soft suppression schemes like Soft-NMS \cite{bodla2017soft}. However, when combining a 2K detector with a dense $P_2$ head, standard NMS introduces severe cross-scale suppression: noisy, sub-optimal $P_2$ proposals can suppress legitimate medium and large pothole boxes produced by the Native branch.

To eliminate cross-scale interference while maintaining ultra-low latency, we introduce \textbf{Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS}. Formally, let $\mathcal{D}_{P2}$ be the candidate set from the auxiliary head. We define a scale-partitioned candidate set $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_{P2}$ filtered by bounding box area:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathcal{D}}_{P2} = \Big\{ d \in \mathcal{D}_{P2} \;\Big|\; \text{Area}(d) < \tau_{area} \;\land\; \text{score}(d) \ge \tau_{conf} \Big\},
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{area} = 32^2 = 1024\text{ px}^2$ enforces a strict ultra-fine prior, and $\tau_{conf} = 0.001$ preserves candidate sensitivity. The unified detection pool is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{fused} = \text{NMS}\big( \mathcal{D}_{Nat} \cup \hat{\mathcal{D}}_{P2}, \;\text{IoU}_{thr} = 0.60 \big).
\end{equation}
By discarding non-tiny $P_2$ proposals prior to NMS, we eliminate $>90\%$ of candidate box comparisons, cutting fusion latency from $9.59\text{ ms}$ to \textbf{4.24~ms per image}.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{The HRP4K Dataset}
All experiments are conducted on the official HRP4K benchmark \cite{hrp4k_dataset}, consisting of 6,003 ultra-high-definition 4K images ($3840 \times 2160\text{ px}$) captured across diverse urban and highway pavements, lighting scenarios, and weather conditions. Following the canonical split, 5,103 images are used for training and validation, and 900 images (containing 921 ground-truth potholes) are reserved exclusively for testing.

The 921 test potholes are partitioned into four standardized area scales:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ultra-Fine} ($S < 32^2 = 1024\text{ px}^2$): 472 instances ($51.25\%$).
    \item \textbf{Fine} ($32^2 \le S < 96^2 = 9216\text{ px}^2$): 169 instances ($18.35\%$).
    \item \textbf{Medium} ($96^2 \le S < 144^2 = 20736\text{ px}^2$): 147 instances ($15.96\%$).
    \item \textbf{Large} ($S \ge 144^2\text{ px}^2$): 133 instances ($14.44\%$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dual-Mode Evaluation Protocols}
To provide unbiased and comprehensive comparisons, we evaluate all models under two distinct protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Academic Protocol} ($\text{conf} = 0.001$): Computes COCO-style metrics ($AP_{50}$, $AP_{75}$, $AP_{50:95}$) across the entire Precision-Recall curve using 101-point interpolation.
    \item \textbf{Operational Protocol} ($\text{conf} = 0.25$): Evaluates real-world vehicular suitability at a fixed confidence threshold of $0.25$, reporting Precision, Recall, $F_1$-score, False Positives Per Image ($\text{FPPI}_{all}$), and inference latency in milliseconds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implementation and Training Details}
The base RT-DETR-L model is pretrained on 2K resolution ($1920 \times 1080$). The auxiliary $P_2$ head is optimized using the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-3}$, cosine learning rate scheduling, and weight decay $1 \times 10^{-4}$ for 30 epochs with early stopping patience of 5 epochs. Experiments are conducted on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs (102 GB VRAM) and RTX 4090 GPUs using PyTorch 2.4 and PyCOCOTools.

\begin{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\section{Ablation Studies and Discussion}

\subsection{Non-Greedy Post-Processing and Fusion Alternatives (Table 4)}
A core question in multi-branch detection is whether greedy NMS can be superseded by advanced ensembling techniques. We evaluate three distinct post-processing alternatives across all 900 test images (540,000 candidate proposals):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS}: Restricting $P_2$ candidate boxes to maximum area thresholds $S < 32^2$, $48^2$, and $64^2\text{ px}^2$.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Calibrated WBF} \cite{solovyev2021weighted}: Clustering cross-branch boxes and computing confidence-weighted coordinate averages.
    \item \textbf{Proposal-Level MLP Gating}: A 2-layer neural network ($9 \to 32 \to 1$, 353 params) evaluated via 2-Fold Disjoint Cross Validation (to guarantee zero data leakage).
\end{enumerate}

The findings in Table~\ref{tab:advanced_fusion_results} reveal vital theoretical insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Why Weighted Boxes Fusion Fails ($AP_{50} < 4\%$)}: WBF presumes that ensemble members share comparable coordinate calibration. In asymmetric multi-resolution branches, Native boxes are precisely localized on 2K coordinates with high confidence ($s \sim 0.7$), whereas dense $P_2$ proposals have uncalibrated scores ($s \sim 0.01$). Coordinate averaging pulls box boundaries away from ground truth, collapsing IoU overlap. Furthermore, the fused confidence score $\frac{\sum s_i}{N}$ dilutes confident Native detections below operational thresholds. WBF is fundamentally unsuited for multi-scale specialized heads.
    \item \textbf{Proposal MLP Gating Trade-off}: The 353-parameter MLP achieves an astonishing \textbf{91.31\% overall Recall} and \textbf{93.43\% Ultra-fine Recall}. However, extreme candidate imbalance ($99.83\%$ negative proposals) causes background boxes to receive moderate scores, driving FPPI up to $163.89$ at fixed threshold $0.25$.
    \item \textbf{Superiority of Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS}: Filtering $P_2$ boxes by $S < 32^2$ achieves the best balance: peak $F_1$-score ($47.22\%$), lowest FPPI ($1.4967$), highest Ultra-Fine $\text{AP}_{50}$ ($50.39\%$), and a $2.26\times$ latency reduction ($4.24\text{ ms}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational Efficiency \& Edge Deployment Feasibility}
In vehicular pavement inspection, real-time edge processing is mandatory. As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:resolution_benchmark} and Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture_pipeline}, P2-DETR adds only $2.98\text{M}$ parameters to RT-DETR-L. On an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, total pipeline latency is $47.4\text{ ms}$ (Backbone: $43.2\text{ ms}$, $P_2$ Head + ScalePart NMS: $4.24\text{ ms}$), delivering an operational throughput of \textbf{21.1 FPS} at 2K resolution. This easily satisfies the $20\text{ FPS}$ threshold for real-time mobile inspection vans traveling at $60\text{ km/h}$.

\section{Conclusion}
In this work, we addressed the fundamental challenge of detecting ultra-fine potholes in 4K road imagery. Through comprehensive multi-paradigm benchmarking on HRP4K, we exposed the \textit{4K Illusion} (brute-force scaling degrades accuracy and quadruples latency) and demonstrated the failure of spatial patch slicing (SAHI incurs multi-second delays and false alarm explosions). To resolve this paradox, we proposed \textbf{P2-DETR}, combining a frozen 2K Transformer detector with a lightweight dense $P_2$ auxiliary head ($stride=4$, $2.98\text{M}$ parameters) trained via Sigmoid Focal Loss. By introducing Scale-Partitioned Prior NMS, we eliminated cross-scale interference, cutting post-processing latency to $4.24\text{ ms}$ and boosting Ultra-Fine Recall to \textbf{93.22\%--94.07\%}. Our work establishes a practical, real-time blueprint for high-resolution vehicular road distress inspection. Future work will explore learned cross-attention feature gating for end-to-end set prediction.
